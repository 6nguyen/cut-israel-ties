# Social platform trends after the 2026 midterms, and what a nationwide tool must route to
Researched: 2026-11-04 · Informs: [docs/prd/action-router.md](../prd/action-router.md)

Snapshot taken the evening of 2026-11-04 (US), about 24 hours after polls closed. Question: what is topical on TikTok and Instagram about the election and actions against the US–Israel partnership right now, which asks are circulating with live tools, and what that implies for a product that serves every US district rather than Orange County. Complements `civic-action-levers/` (which is CA-centric); does not supersede it. Items marked **VERIFY** were not confirmed.

## Method and its limits
- Public, unauthenticated views only: TikTok `/discover/<topic>` pages (public, indexed, no dates on posts), Instagram `/explore/tags/<tag>` (only some tags render logged-out; `#freepalestine` and `#callyourreps` hit a login wall), TikTok Creative Center top-hashtags (public, but surfaces commercial tags only; no politics), Google Trends "Trending now" US 24h/48h, and web search over news published 2026-11-03/04.
- Platform view counts below are the counts shown on the page at snapshot time, not measured engagement over a window.
- Nothing here is a hashtag-volume time series. For that, a logged-in TikTok Creative Center or a social-listening tool is needed.

## 3. Audience evidence
- Yale Youth Poll (spring 2026): voters whose news source is TikTok (41%), Instagram (39%), X (38%), Reddit (39%) are the most likely to agree with at least one strongly anti-Israel statement; ages 18–22 at 55% on "end slavish surrender", vs 35% of all voters. Salience is low: Israel/Palestine is the least-selected priority (28%), 38% among young Democrats.
- Cygnal (Oct 2026): TikTok-news users 34% more likely than average to oppose Israel.
- NBC (Apr 2026): three-quarters of Gen Z sympathize more with Palestinians.
- Harvard Youth Poll (spring 2026): only 35% of young people said they were "likely" to vote in November. Attitude is not behaviour.

## 4. Findings for a nationwide product (findings, not design)
1. **The viral unit this week is a person beating AIPAC, not a policy.** El-Sayed, Mamdani, Biss, "the good guys won". Anger with a named target plus a visible win travels; "Section 1217" does not. A product that wants reach should let users attach their action to a named member and a scoreboard, in the grammar people already share (Track AIPAC cards).
2. **Trump's aid announcement is not what people are searching or posting.** Its effect is to move the fight from "$3.8B grants" to "what replaces them": FMS, the NDAA integration section, the 2028 MOU. Copy that says "end aid" is already stale; the ask is "no sales, no merger, no new MOU".
3. **Nothing routes a nationwide user to a live vote.** "Call your reps" on TikTok is evergreen, generic, ceasefire-era, and points at 5 Calls. Adalah's tool is the only live call tool on a dated decision and it has no script, no count, no per-member status. Indivisible's Schumer tool is reused 2025 tooling. The gap identified in `civic-action-levers/organizing-channels-and-tools.md` holds nationally.
4. **Three asks are live in every state or district in the next 45 days, and they are different levers for different users:**
   - NDAA §219/§1217 conference: every House member and senator; Armed Services members and conferees weigh most. (House: 435 targets; Senate: 100.)
   - Senate Democratic leader vote (December): only states with a Democratic senator; ask is a public "no" or conditions.
   - House Democratic caucus vote Nov 18–19: only districts with a Democratic member; secondary (see `speaker-vote-context.md`).
   After January: FY28 appropriations (next Massie-type amendment), Block the Bombs floor vote demand, Senate JRDs on any new sale.
5. **Nationwide routing needs per-member and per-state facts, most of which already exist in public datasets:** Roll Call 243 vote (House), April 2026 JRD votes (Senate), Armed Services membership, Block the Bombs cosponsorship, Khanna-letter signature (**not published**), AIPAC/UDP money (Track AIPAC, FEC), 2028 primary-target status (American Priorities list), state pension/treasury Israel-bond holdings (Break the Bonds coalitions, state CAFRs), city divestment ordinances (AFSC tracker). A user in a Republican district still has two senators, a state treasurer, and a 2028 list to check.
6. **Distribution reality.** TikTok discover pages are public and indexed by search engines: a page per member or per vote with a stable slug can rank. Instagram hashtag pages are login-walled: distribution there is creators and shareable cards, not tags. TikTok's Creative Center does not surface political hashtags. The research in `behavioral-science-and-virality.md` (deep links into own groups, receipts of hard actions, nominate three) still applies; Adalah already uses "send to three people".
7. **Backlash is as viral as celebration.** Any member-level scoreboard will be screenshotted by the other side (Bongino-style "threat" content, Bulwark-style methodology attacks). Every per-member claim needs a dated primary source on the row, or the product becomes the story.
