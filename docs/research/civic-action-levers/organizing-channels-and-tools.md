# Organizing channels and tools
Researched: 2026-11-04 · Informs: none yet

## What exists today for this cause

- **National groups with live campaigns**: Justice Democrats (primary endorsements, "not a dime from AIPAC"), DSA (5–6 House members elected 2026), IMEU Policy Project (polling, pressure statements), FCNL (legislative), JVP / IfNotNow (direct action; JVP-RI led Providence bonds ordinance), Uncommitted, AFSC (divestment tracker and target list), RootsAction / Peace Action (petitions against Schumer/Jeffries), Sunrise (expanded mission; primaries), Indivisible (leadership fight aimed at Schumer; Signal/WhatsApp organizing training).
- **No organized WhatsApp/Signal/Discord channel** was found for the House speaker fight. For the Israel-aid fight, the above groups run their own lists; none found that routes by district to a specific vote with a public whip count.
- **The Change.org "choose a new speaker" petition**: started Sept 3, 2026 by a paid "Change Ambassador" (Change.org pays creators with 100k+ followers $0.30–$0.50/signature plus an advance); ~72.8k signatures; no delivery plan, no linked groups. A monetized content product, not an organizing vehicle.

## Case studies: online → offline mobilization

### Brazil, truckers' strike (May 2018)
- One of the largest WhatsApp-enabled mobilizations ever; immobilized the country ~10 days.
- Groups capped at 256; truckers were in several overlapping groups, which spread messages fast.
- Weakness: no identifiable leadership. Government "had to identify the leadership at the base of the movement" (15 emergent reps) to negotiate.

### Brazil, PEC da Blindagem (Sept 2025) — closest analog to a vote-pressure campaign
- Lower house passed an amendment shielding legislators from prosecution (secret ballot, Sept 16).
- Protests called in under a week; held in all 27 state capitals and interior cities on Sept 21; hundreds of thousands.
- CUT's "Na Pressão" tool let users message each senator by WhatsApp/email/social; volume crashed the platform.
- Senate CCJ rejected unanimously Sept 24 — three days later — which archived the bill without a floor vote.
- Pattern: street action + direct per-legislator contact + hard deadline, in the same week.

### Brazil, "Exército do Lula" (Oct 2026)
- WhatsApp **channel** (one-way broadcast), created by Deputy André Janones; ~50k at start of runoff → 10.8M by election night (~3 weeks).
- Run as a "digital command centre": daily missions, a commanding voice, an emotional cause, clear objectives.
- Converted online participation into interpersonal persuasion, street mobilization, turnout (e.g., supporters offered car rides on election day where transit was restricted).
- Caveat: whether it delivered votes is not established.

### Kenya, Finance Bill 2024
- Tools: Finance Bill GPT (Kelvin Onkundi) explained the bill and surfaced MPs' phone numbers; a USSD code (*665*971#) gave meetup points, facts, "Ask Finance GPT," anonymous tips; Corrupt Politicians GPT; a "wall of shame" website; crowdsourced MP phone numbers via Google Sheets and messaging apps.
- MPs received thousands of messages daily; many switched phones off; number-sharing violated the Data Protection Act.
- Outcome: Parliament passed the bill anyway (195–106); withdrawn only after storming of Parliament and ~50 deaths; 97% re-passed quietly five months later.

### Israel, judicial-overhaul protests (2023)
- WhatsApp used for neighborhood "micro-organizing"; made protest easier and leadership harder.

### India, farmers' protest (2020–21)
- WhatsApp for fundraising and transport; government agents joined planning groups.

### Patterns
1. WhatsApp is good for speed and reach, bad for decisions. Winners had identifiable people who could negotiate.
2. Admins become de facto leaders; choose them deliberately.
3. Layered structure: one announcement channel + small local groups.
4. Assume infiltration and misinformation; anything posted is public.
5. Tie the message to a specific ask, target, and deadline.
6. WhatsApp platform limits: forward to ≤5 chats; an already-forwarded message to 1 chat; "forwarded many times" label after 5 hops; the 2020 limit cut highly-forwarded messages 70%. Spread runs through groups (≤512), broadcast lists (≤256), Communities, channels, and links — not forward chains.

## Existing civic tools and evidence

| Tool | What it does | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **5 Calls** (5calls.org) | Zip → reps, script, auto-dial; curated topic list | >7M calls by early 2025, ~3M post-inauguration; Senate switchboard hit ~1,600 calls/min vs 40 normal (Murkowski, Feb 2025); staffer: "The calls are working" (DeVos fight). Calls are tallied for/against; CMF rates calls below personalized letters. |
| **Resistbot** (text RESIST to 50409) | Converts texts to letters/faxes to all reps | Hit 1M pages/month; some offices unplugged fax machines; now requires users to write their own message. |
| **Na Pressão** (CUT, Brazil; napressao.org.br) | Per-legislator page with WhatsApp/email/Facebook/Instagram/X buttons; legislators sorted into for / undecided / against columns | ~100k visits in first 10 days (2019); most pressure went to undecided via WhatsApp; 2025 campaign crashed the platform; CUT credits it with delaying a Senate vote (PEC 66) and with the PEC da Blindagem defeat. Key design: visible whip count + per-target direct channel. |
| **Finance Bill GPT / USSD** (Kenya) | Bill explainer + MP numbers + meetup points | Massive adoption; bill still passed. |
| **Change.org** | Petitions | Monetized via Change Ambassadors; CMF: form communications least effective. |
| **CivicView, citizensunited.app, Elect Connect** (2026 "vibe-coded" civic apps) | Verified-constituent polls; public receipts; 14-day silence visibility | Unproven; verification "coming soon." |
| **Mobilize, Action Network** | Event/list infrastructure used by Indivisible etc. | Standard backend; not differentiated. |

## Congressional Management Foundation findings (what moves an undecided member)
- In-person constituent visits: 94–99% of staff say "some" or "a lot" of influence.
- Personalized postal letters 90%; personalized email 88%; phone calls 84%; form emails far lower.
- Personal story related to the issue: 79% say helpful; only 18% receive it frequently.
- "When you're yelling, Congress isn't listening." Specific request is key.
- Trained constituents: 97% rated "very prepared" vs 12% typical.

## Gap this product could fill
Nothing combines Na Pressão's public whip count with US per-district constituent contact for a named vote. Minimum viable: one row per Democratic House member — position on the vote, source link, office numbers, one-line script, a tracker of whether the office responded — sorted by "uncommitted."
