# Levers and actions, scored
Researched: 2026-11-04 · Informs: [docs/prd/vote-tracker.md](../../prd/vote-tracker.md)

Goal: end US aid, ties, and military partnership with Israel at federal, state, and local levels. Reference user location: Orange, CA (city split between CA-40 and CA-46 after Prop 50).

## Executive summary

1. **The money flow is already ending on its own timeline; the fight that remains is what replaces it.** The $3.8B/yr MOU expires Sept 2028. Formal talks on a replacement began May 2026. Trump said (Nov 3, 2026) grants stop at end of 2026. Netanyahu wants "aid to partnership." The replacement is NDAA Section 219/224 (defense-technology integration) plus foreign military sales. A product aimed at "aid" alone is fighting last year's battle.
2. **The single highest-impact citizen lever is electoral — Democratic primaries.** The July 15, 2026 House vote (103 Democrats to end aid) happened because members fear primaries. AIPAC's deterrent weakened: ~$55.3M of its $84.1M 2026 spend went to losing races — though 92% of its backed candidates still won, mostly safe incumbents.
3. **Second-highest: named votes with dates.** Members respond to constituent pressure tied to a specific roll call. Live now: NDAA conference (Section 224), FY27 appropriations, Senate Democratic leader vote (December). Pressure without a scheduled vote dissipates.
4. **State and local money is real but small; the win condition is institutional, not legislative.** Maryland's pension sold 85% of its Israel bonds ($62M) after ~1 year of campaigning. CalSTRS reportedly holds ~$2.7B in targeted companies plus Israel bonds. UC holds $32B and the Regents refuse to divest. City divestments (Hayward $1.6M, Pasadena, Richmond) are symbolic in dollar terms.
5. **Lowest impact per unit of attention: petitions, form emails, consumer boycotts, city resolutions.** Boycott effects on McDonald's/Starbucks were real in the Middle East but unverifiable as to attribution, and sales recovered.
6. **Effort and impact are inversely correlated.** What moves outcomes (primary organizing, in-person constituent meetings, pension-board campaigns) requires showing up. From-home actions have moderate impact only when aimed at a specific member and vote.
7. **Location determines everything.** A CA-46 resident (Correa, voted to keep aid) has a federal target. A CA-40 resident (Republican) has almost none federally and should be routed to state/county/institutional actions. The product's core value is this routing.

## Priority order (where the needle moves)

1. Primaries in safe Democratic seats (CA-46 is one).
2. Three named votes in the next 60 days: NDAA conference (Sec. 224), Senate Democratic leader, appropriations.
3. CalSTRS/CalPERS — the only California-controlled pool large enough to be noticed; public-employee users have standing beyond "constituent."
4. Everything else is list-building.
