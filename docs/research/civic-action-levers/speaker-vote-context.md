# Speaker vote context (why leadership pressure isn't the lever)
Researched: 2026-11-04 · Informs: none yet

## Timeline
- Nov 3, 2026: Democrats win House (≥250–258 seats projected) and Senate.
- Nov 18–19: House Democratic caucus leadership elections, closed-door, secret ballot. Picks nominee for Speaker, Majority Leader, Whip, etc.
- Nov 10: House/Senate Republican leadership elections. Johnson not running for minority leader.
- December: Senate Democratic leadership election (Schumer).
- Jan 3, 2027: Speaker elected by name on the House floor.

## Findings
- No challenger to Jeffries exists or is preparing. Clark, Aguilar, AOC, Mamdani, New Dems all endorsed him. "I haven't lost a single Democratic vote yet" (20 ballots).
- Uncommitted bloc: 5–6 DSA-endorsed freshmen (Avila Chevalier, Valdez, Kiros, McKinney, Rabb) plus an unknown share of the ~80 candidates who were noncommittal pre-election. None has said they'd vote against him; they're using the vote to extract policy commitments (Avila Chevalier's list: voting rights, public housing, blocking offensive weapons to Israel, universal healthcare; Kiros: corporate-PAC standard).
- Math: ~130 "no" votes needed in a ~258-member caucus on a secret ballot; on the floor he can lose 35+ and still win.
- 2018 precedent: 16-member public letter against Pelosi with a ~17-seat majority produced a term-limits deal, a no-retaliation pledge, and a leadership-development program — not a new Speaker. Term limits were never ratified. Her team said she had the votes anyway.
- Candidates don't emerge from outside pressure; they emerge when a leader looks beatable and a challenger's upside exceeds the cost of losing (committee assignments, access).
- Realistic asks for the Nov 18 window: public commitments in exchange for votes (rules, committee seats, floor votes on specific bills, no-retaliation), a public "no" letter with 30–40 names to make a January challenge thinkable, revival of leadership term limits.
- Schumer is not in this race (Senate). His leadership vote is in December; several incoming senators have said they won't back him; "before there's a challenge, people have to step forward."

## Orange, CA routing
- City split between CA-40 (Republican-leaning after Prop 50; Calvert vs Kim) and CA-46 (Correa, D, ~68%). Confirm by full street address at house.gov.
- CA-46 constituents have a caucus vote to pressure; CA-40 constituents have none.
