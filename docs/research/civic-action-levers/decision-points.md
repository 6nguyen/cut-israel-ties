# Decision points and context
Researched: 2026-11-04 · Informs: [docs/prd/vote-tracker.md](../../prd/vote-tracker.md)

## Federal: the five decision points and who owns each

### 1. The annual $3.3B FMF + $500M missile defense
- Appropriated yearly; the MOU is not legally binding.
- House vote, July 15, 2026 (Massie amendment to the National Security/State approps bill, H.R. 8595, Roll Call 243): failed 104–314, 10 present. Democrats 103–98–10. Massie the only Republican yes.
- Jeffries sent a Dear Colleague saying he'd vote No (amendment "overly broad"), did not whip, and called for renegotiating the next MOU; said he is open to phasing out direct military aid after 2028 and shifting from grants to weapons sales.
- Clark (No. 2 Democrat) and Pelosi voted Aye. Aguilar, Lieu, DelBene voted No.
- California delegation (partial, from Clerk): Aguilar No; Barragán Aye; Bera Present; Brownley Aye; Carbajal Aye; Chu Aye; Cisneros No; **Correa No**; Costa No; DeSaulnier Aye; Friedman Aye; Garamendi Aye; Gomez No; Gray No; Huffman Present.
- AIPAC responded by closing its donation portal to the 15–18 endorsees who voted Aye, without formally rescinding endorsements.
- Separate vote: MTG amendment to cut $500M missile defense failed 422–6.
- Owner: each member's vote, then the Speaker's control (via Rules) of which amendments reach the floor.

### 2. The next MOU
- Current MOU: FY2019–FY2028, $38B ($33B FMF + $5B missile defense), expires Sept 2028.
- Formal replacement talks began May/June 2026 (Israeli MoD).
- Trump, Nov 3, 2026 (Fox): "$4 billion... gets cut off at the end of the year." Netanyahu has talked about tapering over a decade, not immediately. AIPAC: "we support the current MOU." No law has been enacted ending MOU-level assistance after 2026.
- Netanyahu's framing: "from aid recipient to partner." Stutzman (R) nonbinding House resolution to end direct aid over 10 years and move to "mutual defense cooperation and joint economic investment."
- Owner: executive negotiates; Congress funds.

### 3. Military integration — NDAA FY2027 Section 219 (House) / 224
- Creates a "United States-Israel Defense Technology Cooperation Initiative" with a DoD executive agent coordinating across nine technology categories (AI, quantum, biotech, etc.). Derived from the US-Israel FUTURES Act (Budd/Gillibrand; Ronny Jackson).
- JINSA takes credit for it; JINSA also pushes a mutual defense treaty, basing US forces in Israel, a new CENTCOM HQ there — none of that is in legislation.
- Khanna called it a "fusion" of the militaries designed to evade scrutiny; his HASC amendment to strip it failed by voice vote (June 4, 2026). House passed the NDAA in July. Senate version has a similar provision. Conference pending.
- AJC's counter: does not place forces under the other's command, create a treaty, or add new assistance; "coordinates existing and future cooperation."
- Owner: NDAA conferees (Armed Services), then floor.

### 4. Arms sales (AECA joint resolutions of disapproval)
- Senate JRDs are privileged — leadership cannot block them.
- Apr 15, 2026: 40 of 47 Democrats voted to block D9 bulldozers; 36 to block 1,000-lb bombs. Schumer voted against. Padilla and Schiff voted yes; Schiff, Wyden, Ossoff, Slotkin newly on bombs. Kelly and Gallego yes.
- Trajectory: Nov 2024, 18–19; Apr 2025, 15; Jul 2025, 27; Apr 2026, 40.
- House JRDs are not privileged — the Speaker decides whether they get a vote.
- Executive can bypass review: Trump administration notified ~$7B (Apache helicopters etc.) with one hour's notice to Schumer, disregarding standing practice.

### 5. Block the Bombs Act
- Restricts certain offensive weapons; 77 cosponsors as of July 2026. No floor vote without Speaker consent.

## Member positions (relevant to Orange, CA)

- **Jeffries (D-NY, incoming Speaker)**: voted to keep aid; calls for "major reset"; open to phase-out after 2028 and shift to sales; wants Gaza reconstruction, Hamas disarmed. AIPAC PAC sent $149,300 earmarked to his joint fundraising committee May 20, 2026 (largest earmarked disbursement in its history); AIPAC has raised >$1M for him.
- **Schumer (D-NY, Senate leader)**: voted against both April JRDs; quoted saying his job is "to fight for aid to Israel." Faces leadership vote in December; several incoming senators (Troy Jackson, Flanagan, McMorrow, Platner) said they won't support him.
- **Correa (D, CA-46)**: voted No on Massie amendment (to keep aid). AIPAC-aligned endorsements in past cycles. Won 2026 with ~68%. Next primary June 2028.
- **Padilla, Schiff (D-CA senators)**: both voted yes on April 2026 JRDs.
- **Aguilar (D, CA-33, caucus chair)**: voted No on Massie.

## Public opinion (2026)

- NYT/Siena May 2026: 74% of Democrats oppose "providing additional economic and military support to Israel"; 20% support. Up from 45% opposed three years earlier.
- NBC May 2026: 13% of Democrats view Israel positively; 57% negatively; 67% sympathize more with Palestinians.
- Economist/YouGov Apr 2026: 4% of Democrats support increasing aid; 56% decrease; 35% stop entirely.
- Pew Apr 2026: 80% of Democrats and 41% of Republicans view Israel negatively (cited by Sanders).
- IGA/YouGov June 2026: 67% of Democrats say the relationship hurts the US more than it helps.
- Poll: 82% of NY Democratic voters oppose weapons transfers (cited by Common Dreams).

## Electoral evidence (2026 cycle)

- AIPAC/UDP spent $84.1M; $55.3M in races where its candidate lost (Responsible Statecraft).
- Its backed candidates won 245 of 267 (92%), mostly safe incumbents; incumbents overall won 91% (whofundsmyrep).
- Dem primaries: AIPAC "winning 3 and losing 4" among contested races; Biss (IL-9) beat $6.3M; El-Sayed (MI Senate) beat $30M; McKinney beat Thanedar (MI-13).
- Illinois: AIPAC super PAC seeded two anonymous groups spending $14M+; won two races (Bean, Miller).
- Five–six DSA-endorsed members elected to the House (Avila Chevalier, Valdez — NY; Kiros — CO; McKinney — MI; Rabb — PA).

## California state level

- **No state "aid" line item.** Exposure is investments and contracting.
- **CalSTRS**: ~$390B fund; DSA campaign says $2.7B in targeted companies (Palantir, Lockheed, Caterpillar, Chevron) plus Israel bonds bought March 2024 (maturing 2034, 2054). Board hears public comment at Investment Committee; no substantive vote taken.
- **CalPERS**: ~$467B; policy opposes non-financial divestment on fiduciary grounds; opposed SB 252 (fossil fuels), which died.
- **2026 divestment bill**: Newsom publicly defended a bill instructing CalPERS/CalSTRS to divest from companies profiting from the conflict (Mar 2026; Fetterman called it antisemitic). **VERIFY** bill number, author, status.
- **AB 2844 (2016)**: contractors certify they don't violate civil-rights law when boycotting a sovereign nation; Israel the only country named. Passed 34–1 / 69–1.
- **Ballot initiatives**: #25-0011 (right to BDS; bars pension restrictions) withdrawn Jan 29, 2026. 2010 CalPERS/CalSTRS divestment initiative failed to qualify (needed ~434k signatures).
- **State Treasurer**: WA State Treasurer reportedly sold $53M+ Caterpillar bonds (BDS claim). CA Treasurer holdings not researched.
- **UC**: $32B in assets protesters target; Regents refuse; only student-government funds have divested (UC Davis ASUCD ~$20M budget).

## County / city precedents (US)

- Hayward CA (2024): divested $1.6M from Chevron, Intel, Hyundai, Caterpillar; modeled on its 1985 South Africa divestment.
- Pasadena CA: divested from weapons, fossil fuels, private prisons.
- Richmond CA: resolution calling for arms embargo and end to US military assistance.
- Urbana IL (June 2026): ethical-investment policy excluding top-100 arms producers and AFSC-listed companies.
- Iowa City (Aug 2025), Northampton MA (Sept 2025, ~$71k), Portland ME, Medford MA: divestment resolutions/ordinances.
- Providence RI: Israel-bonds divestment ordinance; mayor threatened veto.
- Montgomery County MD (July 2026): dropped funding for Maryland/Israel Development Center.
- Maryland pension (May 2026): sold 85% of Israel bonds ($62M), $11M remaining.
- Police exchanges: Durham NC barred military-style training abroad; JINSA sent Irvine and Garden Grove chiefs (2000s); NY sent 13 officers Sept 2025.
- Unions: UAW convention June 2026 barred Israel bonds in its constitution.

## Opposing positions (for evenhanded product copy)

- AIPAC: "Security assistance is critical to Israel's security and the U.S.-Israel alliance... It has ensured Israel can defend itself."
- AJC on Sec. 219: not new assistance; no merging of command; preserves independent decision-making.
- Jeffries on Massie amendment: "overly broad"; could threaten the US embassy, peace-building, and humanitarian aid to Palestinians.
- CalPERS/CalSTRS: fiduciary duty bars non-financial divestment; SB 252 divestment estimated to cost $75–125M.
